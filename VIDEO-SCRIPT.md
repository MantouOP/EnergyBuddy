# EnergyBuddy — 4 minute 30 second video script

This script stays under the five-minute limit. Rehearse once with a timer and keep the demo moving.

## 0:00–0:25 — Hook and sign-in

**Visual:** Start on the EnergyBuddy login screen, then use **Continue with Google — demo**.

“A student can have two free hours and still have no energy left to use them. Calendars show where time goes, but not the mental, physical and social cost of everything we carry. EnergyBuddy makes that invisible load visible before burnout feels sudden.”

## 0:25–0:55 — Problem and user

**Visual:** Show Today, its commitments and the three batteries.

“Our user is a university student balancing classes, assignments, paid work, commuting and social responsibilities. Their plans are fragmented, their capacity is invisible, and stopping to rest can feel like failure. We wanted to give that student an early warning and a practical, guilt-free response.”

## 0:55–1:30 — Three batteries and check-in

**Visual:** Point to Mental, Physical and Social energy, then the check-in.

“EnergyBuddy separates mental, physical and social energy instead of hiding everything inside one wellness score. A fifteen-second check-in keeps the estimate personal without creating another journaling chore. This is not a medical diagnosis; it is an explainable workload-risk estimate.”

## 1:30–2:05 — Seven-day forecast

**Action:** Open **Energy Forecast**.

“The forecast turns the coming week into familiar weather. Thursday is Stormy: the student starts with sixty-one percent energy but faces eighty-five percent demand. EnergyBuddy shows the reason—two deep-work blocks and a late shift with no recovery between them.”

## 2:05–2:55 — What-If planner

**Action:** Open **What-If Planner**, compare both plans, then click **Apply balanced plan**.

“Awareness alone is not enough. What-If Mode keeps the essential lab and paid shift, splits the assignment, protects lunch and moves flexible revision. The student can inspect every change before accepting it. Thursday then improves from a seven-percent Storm reserve to thirty-one percent and Cloudy—without removing an essential commitment.”

## 2:55–3:55 — Proactive Rest

**Action:** Open **Proactive Rest**. Complete a quest, select **40 min**, then start the environment check-in.

“Our newest idea makes rest a first-class KPI. Rest Quests turn vague advice into specific actions: step outside, take a no-phone reset or close your eyes for twenty minutes. Completing recovery increases the Rest score.”

“The Recharge Curve gives students a logical reason to stop. In this scenario, forty minutes of rest may raise estimated evening coding focus from thirty to seventy-five percent. The number is transparent and adjustable, not medical advice.”

“The environment check-in is a prototype simulation. A future mobile build could use HealthKit, Google Fit, steps or location—with permission—to confirm that the student genuinely left the study zone.”

## 3:55–4:20 — Novelty and feasibility

**Visual:** Keep the Proactive Rest dashboard visible.

“Todo lists organise work, while self-care apps suggest recovery. EnergyBuddy connects both: it predicts energy weather, explains overload, tests schedule changes and rewards deliberate rest. The web prototype uses React and transparent rules; secure storage and permission-based mobile sensors are clearly separated into the build phase.”

## 4:20–4:30 — Close

**Visual:** Return to the updated forecast or Rest KPI.

“Calendars tell students when they are busy. EnergyBuddy helps them see when they are running out—and makes rest part of the plan.”

## Recording checklist

- Record at 1080p with browser zoom around 100%.
- Close notifications and unrelated tabs.
- Use the exact order: Login → Today → Forecast → What-If → Apply → Proactive Rest.
- Keep the final cut between 3:00 and 5:00.
- Upload as **Unlisted**, not Private.
- Test the video link while signed out before submission.
